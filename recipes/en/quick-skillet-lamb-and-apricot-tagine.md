I absolutely love how this tagine comes together so quickly, making it a perfect weeknight wonder. It has all the warming, aromatic flavors of a traditional tagine, but with a fraction of the time. The combination of tender lamb, sweet apricots, and fragrant spices is truly magical, and I find it incredibly comforting. Even if you're new to cooking or haven't made a tagine before, don't be intimidated; this skillet version is straightforward and forgiving.

## Ingredients

- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 lb boneless lamb shoulder, cut into 1-inch cubes
- 1 large onion, chopped
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp ground coriander
- 1/2 tsp ground ginger
- 1/4 tsp turmeric
- 1/4 tsp cinnamon
- Pinch of cayenne pepper (optional)
- 1 (14.5 oz) can diced tomatoes, undrained
- 1 cup chicken or vegetable broth
- 1/2 cup dried apricots, halved
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper to taste
- Fresh cilantro or parsley, chopped, for garnish
- Cooked couscous or rice, for serving

## Instructions

1. Heat olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Season lamb cubes generously with salt and pepper. Add lamb to the skillet and brown on all sides, working in batches if necessary to avoid crowding the pan. Remove lamb from skillet and set aside.
2. Add the chopped onion to the same skillet and cook until softened, about 5-7 minutes. Add the minced garlic and cook for another minute until fragrant.
3. Stir in the cumin, coriander, ginger, turmeric, cinnamon, and cayenne pepper (if using). Cook for 30 seconds, stirring constantly, until fragrant.
4. Pour in the diced tomatoes and chicken broth, scraping up any browned bits from the bottom of the skillet. Bring to a simmer.
5. Return the browned lamb to the skillet. Add the halved dried apricots. Season with salt and pepper.
6. Reduce heat to low, cover the skillet, and simmer for 20-25 minutes, or until the lamb is tender and cooked through.
7. Taste and adjust seasoning as needed.
8. Serve the tagine hot over cooked couscous or rice, garnished with fresh cilantro or parsley.
